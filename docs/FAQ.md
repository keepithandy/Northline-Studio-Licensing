# Licensing and Intellectual-Property FAQ

This page is educational documentation, not legal advice. It does not replace the controlling project or file terms.

## Are source code and creative assets licensed the same way?

No. Source code follows the license expressly included with the relevant project or file. Creative assets follow [the creative-content terms](../ASSETS_LICENSE.md) unless a more specific notice applies.

## May I fork or modify a repository?

Only to the extent permitted by that repository's controlling source-code license. A source-code license does not automatically grant rights to logos, artwork, writing, lore, screenshots, audio, or other creative content.

## May I use screenshots or project branding?

Not automatically. Screenshots may contain reserved creative content and third-party material. Review the project terms and submit a permission request when the desired use is not expressly granted.

## May I use a project commercially?

Commercial permissions depend on the controlling source-code, creative-content, trademark, and third-party terms. Submit a [licensing request](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/licensing-request.yml) for permissions not expressly granted.

## Is attribution always enough?

No. Attribution does not replace permission. Follow the exact controlling license and preserve required notices recorded in the [attribution template](THIRD_PARTY_ATTRIBUTION_TEMPLATE.md).

## What about mods and adaptations?

Code changes may be allowed by a code license while creative assets remain restricted. Review all applicable layers before distribution.

## What are third-party materials?

Libraries, fonts, art, audio, tools, and other material supplied by someone else remain governed by their original terms. Record them with the attribution template.

## How are copyright, trademark, and software licensing different?

Copyright protects original expression; trademarks identify the source of goods or services; software licenses grant specified permissions under stated conditions. A grant in one area does not automatically grant rights in the others.

## Which document controls a conflict?

See [Conflict and Precedence Examples](CONFLICT_PRECEDENCE.md). Project-specific, file-specific, and third-party terms control over this repository's summaries where applicable.
